# Production pipeline — JIJI IN MOTION (Posta 2040)

Everything is generated by code and runs headless on a 4-core CPU container (no GPU).

|-------|------|-------------|

| Realistic trees at the 829 real tree positions | Python / bmesh, Pillow textures | `scripts/assets/build_trees.py`, `place_trees.py` → `blender/city_trees.blend` |

## The binary transformation system (`GN_BinaryTransform`)

One reusable node group drives every transformation: buildings, roads, trees, vehicles and humans, in both directions.

Inputs: **Progress** (0 real → 1 binary; animate 1 → 0 for binary → real), **Density**, **Glyph Size**, **Noise Scale**,
**Seed**, **Flow** (time, driven by the frame), **Height Bias** (0 = pure noise, 1 = bottom-to-top sweep), **Band**
(width of the glowing transition front), **Edge Glow**, **Glyphs** (collection holding the `0` and `1` meshes).

Per evaluated surface point a threshold `t = noise·(1−hb) + height·hb` is computed:

1. Faces with `t < Progress` are deleted, so the real surface actually breaks apart.
2. A face attribute `front` marks faces just ahead of the cut; the surface material reads it and glows (turquoise for
   buildings, blue for roads, orange for construction).
3. Points distributed on the *original* surface become `0`/`1` glyph instances where `t < Progress + Band`, aligned to the
   surface normal, scaled in as the front passes, flipping bit and drifting along the normal over time.
4. The original edges become thin glowing tubes, so the structural skeleton remains readable.

Because it runs after the Armature modifier on rigged characters, the glyphs follow the walk cycle.

## Geographic fidelity

The OSM export is never edited (`assets/city/original_map/`, SHA-256 in `CHECKSUMS.sha256`). The 2040 city only changes
building *height and appearance*: every tower stands on its real footprint, every road and the coastline are unchanged,
and trees stand at their real OSM positions. See `03_MAP_COMPARISON.md`.

## Render settings

1280×720, 24 fps, Cycles CPU, 16 samples adaptive with OpenImageDenoise, AgX Medium High Contrast. Every second frame is
rendered and FFmpeg motion-interpolates back to 24 fps. Atmosphere uses a mist pass in the compositor instead of
volumetrics in city shots; real water volume, caustics and light shafts are used underwater.
